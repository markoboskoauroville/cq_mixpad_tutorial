Marko, here is what I did to get the venue mix onto your USB fader. Your CQ twelve T talks to the Mac over one USB cable. That cable carries sixteen channels in each direction. Sixteen go from the mixer to the computer, for recording. Sixteen come back from the computer to the mixer, for playback. Ableton Live calls the ones coming back to the mixer Ext. Out one to sixteen. The mixer decides where each of those sixteen lands. Channels one to ten land on your ten input channels, when an input's source is set to SD and USB instead of analogue. That is how Svemir's voice, the bass and the drums reach faders one to ten. The last six come in three stereo pairs. Eleven and twelve land on the ST IN channel. Thirteen and fourteen land on the channel called USB. Fifteen and sixteen are the main mix. I did not know that from the manual, so I measured it. I opened CQ MixPad, went to the processing page and the bank called ST IN and FX, where you see ST IN, USB and BT side by side. Then I sent the venue track in Live to output fifteen, sixteen, thirteen and eleven, one at a time, and watched which little meter moved. Only thirteen moved the USB meter. So the venue mix track now goes to Ext. Out thirteen. The USB fader stays down at off. That keeps the venue sound out of your main mix and out of the SD recording. To hear it, press the headphone key on the USB channel. The headphone key is listen: it puts that channel alone in your headphones, even with its fader down. Press it again and you are back to your own mix. One more thing. Right now Live sends the venue mix in mono, because only single outputs are switched on in Live. When we switch on thirteen and fourteen as a stereo pair in Live's audio settings, you will hear it in stereo. And the numbers seventeen and eighteen are not on your CQ at all. They were the inputs of the Behringer mixer at the concert, where the venue mix was recorded.
